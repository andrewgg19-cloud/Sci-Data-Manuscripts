\newcommand{\abstracttext}{The objective was to test whether particle-species rest masses, spins, and charges, combined with present cosmological abundance, permit reconstruction of baryon and antibaryon populations during the annihilation epoch and comparison with the literature. An ideal hadron resonance gas with fixed PDG2014, PDG2020, and PDG2025 inventories was used, retaining their masses. A temperature of 50 MeV was adopted as an approximate literature reference, and the baryon chemical potential was solved using the observed excess per entropy unit while maintaining $\mu_Q=\mu_S=0$. Roots were located, thermal sums were verified with independent integrals, and uniqueness of the net map was examined. At 50 MeV, PDG2025 gave $2.7261\times10^{30}$ baryons and $2.7172\times10^{30}$ antibaryons per cm$^3$, with an excess of $8.8765\times10^{27}$ cm$^{-3}$. The antibaryon-to-excess ratio was 306.1147 and the nucleon contribution was 95.2176\%. The criterion equating antibaryons with net excess was reached at 38.203739 MeV, which rounds to the published value of 38.2 MeV. Varying only $\eta$ within its input uncertainty gave 38.192814--38.214598 MeV. The comoving comparison conserved the residual and related total populations to a region with an explicit present volume. Reconstruction was possible under stated temperature, equilibrium, and conservation conditions; the present alone did not identify a unique history. The chemical simplification left nonzero net charge and strangeness. The results provide a reproducible equilibrium reference whose full historical scope requires additional chemical balances and kinetic evolution.}
