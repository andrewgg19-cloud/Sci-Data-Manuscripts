Re: Symmetric Evaporation and Entropy Dilution of the Baryon Excess between 50 and 30 MeV

Dear Editors,

I submit this manuscript for consideration as a research article in The European Physical Journal Plus.

The study examines how a specified, baryon-symmetric energy injection changes plasma entropy and baryon-antibaryon abundance crossings between 50 and 30 MeV. A hypothetical population of nonrotating black holes is coupled to a homogeneous expanding bath and an effective production-annihilation equation.

Its main comparison separates two boundary conditions: a common initial net-baryon yield and a common final yield. These produce opposite directions of crossing-temperature shifts in the tested scenarios. A complete PDG2025 thermal inventory is compared with the reduced bath, and a local mass scan retains a trajectory with three abundance crossings. The manuscript reports reproduction of 36 archived CSV outputs and numerical tolerance and grid checks.

The work is an exploratory computational study under stated assumptions. It preserves comoving net baryon number and does not derive the origin of the baryon asymmetry. Instantaneous homogeneous thermalization, an effective nucleonic rate, and a bath without feedback from the kinetic composition limit the physical interpretation. Separate equilibrium neutrality controls are not presented as a fully coupled chemical evolution.

The preprint and reproducibility supplement are available at https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23467637. The related equilibrium study, “Baryon-Antibaryon Reconstruction at 50 MeV: Rest Masses and Cosmological Abundances,” is deposited at https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.23467572. That manuscript supplies the equilibrium baseline; the present work focuses on energy injection, boundary conditions and effective kinetic evolution. I disclose the shared inputs and companion work for editorial assessment. AI assistance is disclosed in the manuscript.

The journal's coverage of theoretical and computational methods is the basis for this submission.
